## 1. Line rasterization rule

**OBSERVED** (`raw/m4_20260828_run01`, family `line_rule`, 8/8 `OK`/`PASS`, byte-
identical run02):

| case | endpoints (pixel coords, 8x8 target) | result |
|---|---|---|
| horizontal | (1.0,4.5)-(7.0,4.5) | columns 1..6 lit at row 4; column 7 (the endpoint) is NOT lit |
| vertical | (4.5,1.0)-(4.5,7.0) | rows 1..6 lit at column 4; row 7 (the endpoint) NOT lit |
| diagonal 45 deg | (1.0,1.0)-(7.0,7.0) | single-pixel staircase (1,1),(2,2)...(6,6); (7,7) NOT lit |
| shallow slope 3/7 | (0.5,0.5)-(7.5,3.5) | row0={0,1}, row1={2,3}, row2={4,5}, row3={6}; matches evaluating the line's y at each column's **pixel-center x-coordinate** and taking the containing row |
| exact-integer y tie (y=4.0) | (1.0,4.0)-(7.0,4.0) | resolves to **row 3** (the lower row) |
| y=3.99 | same, y=3.99 | row 3 (same as the exact tie) |
| y=4.01 | same, y=4.01 | row 4 |
| degenerate (both endpoints identical) | (4.0,4.0)-(4.0,4.0) | **zero pixels lit** -- a zero-length line rasterizes nothing |

**INTERPRETED.** Every tested case matches a single consistent rule: **half-open
interval, endpoint excluded** (the classic convention that avoids double-drawing a
shared vertex between adjacent segments of a line strip), with per-column row
assignment equal to evaluating the line equation at the pixel-center x and flooring
to the containing row. The row-boundary tie test (y=4.0 exactly vs. y=3.99 vs.
y=4.01) shows the crossover sits at or just above y=4.0, resolving the exact
integer value to the LOWER row (i.e. the row interval is effectively `(r, r+1]` at
this boundary, not `[r, r+1)`) -- recorded precisely as observed rather than forced
into either textbook convention. This experiment did NOT perform a fine (sub-
1/256-pixel) bisection of the hardware's rasterization subpixel grid; a `y=4.001`
probe during exploration (not in the frozen matrix) gave the same row as `y=4.0`,
consistent with subpixel snapping coarser than 0.01px, but the exact snap
granularity is **not resolved** here and is flagged as a narrow open item, not
claimed. A zero-length line degenerates to **no rasterized pixels at all** (neither
a single point nor a fault) -- a driver need not special-case degenerate lines
beyond letting them naturally rasterize nothing.

**Driver consequence:** implement line rasterization as evaluate-at-pixel-center +
floor, exclusive of the final vertex, matching the common OpenGL/D3D convention;
no diamond-exit-specific tie-break beyond "exact integer ties round down" was
observed or needed to be modeled separately.

## 2. Point rasterization and its rounding rule

**OBSERVED** (family `point_rounding`, 16/16 `OK`/`PASS`, point centered exactly at
a true pixel center (16.5,16.5) in a 32x32 target to remove the center-alignment
ambiguity a target-midpoint placement would introduce):

| requested `[[point_size]]` | footprint side | pixel count |
|---|---|---|
| 0.5, 0.9, 1.0, 1.1, 1.4, 1.5, 1.6, 1.9 | **1** | 1 |
| **2.0 exactly** | **2** | 4 |
| 2.1, 2.4, 2.5, 2.6, 2.9, 3.0, 3.5 | **3** | 9 |

**INTERPRETED.** For every tested size strictly less than 2.0, the footprint is a
single pixel (the one containing the point center) -- there is no discard for small
positive sizes (0.5 still renders 1 pixel) when the point sits on a genuine pixel
center; this differs from EXP-0097's target-midpoint-anchored 0.5 case, which
discarded, because a target's exact geometric midpoint on an even-dimensioned
render target is NOT a pixel center (it is the shared corner of 4 pixels) -- the
two results are consistent once the anchoring difference is accounted for, not
contradictory. For every size in (2.0, 3.5], the footprint is 3x3. **Exactly at
size=2.0, the footprint is anomalously 2x2**, breaking the odd-number pattern the
sizes on either side follow (1 -> 3 with no 2 in between at any OTHER tested size).
This is a genuine boundary artifact, not a measurement error (both official runs
agree bit-for-bit). No unifying "round to nearest odd, with a tie exception at even
integers" formula was independently re-derived from first principles here; the
table above is the exact, hardware-confirmed ground truth and should be used
directly rather than a closed-form approximation. **Driver consequence:** a point
sprite implementation must reproduce this exact non-monotonic-in-parity table (or
accept that pointSize=2.0 is a documented irregular case) rather than assuming
`ceil`/`round`/`floor` of the requested size.

(EXP-0097 already fully covers the point-size **clamp ceiling** (511px), NaN/Inf
clamping, and the negative-size anomaly band; not re-derived here.)

## 3. Polygon (fill) modes

**OBSERVED** (family `polygon_fillmode`, 3/3 `OK`/`PASS`; same 16x16-target,
fixed reference triangle `(-0.8,-0.8),(0.8,-0.8),(0.0,0.8)` in every case):

- `MTLTriangleFillModeFill`: **72** lit pixels (solid interior).
- `MTLTriangleFillModeLines`: **38** lit pixels (edges only, `<` the fill count).
- `MTLTriangleFillModeLines` applied while drawing a **line**-topology primitive:
  executes without error or observable difference from the fill-mode-unset
  baseline (fill mode is a triangle-rasterization-only state, inert for lines, as
  its name implies -- recorded as a functioning no-op, not tested further).

**INTERPRETED.** `MTLTriangleFillMode` is a real, functionally distinct, two-valued
enum (`.fill` / `.lines`) confirmed on hardware. **There is no third "points" mode**
in the public API (Vulkan's `VK_POLYGON_MODE_POINT` / GL's `GL_POINT` polygon
mode) -- this is a structural fact from the SDK header (the enum has exactly two
cases), not independently probed by feeding an out-of-range raw integer to
`setTriangleFillMode:` (flagged as a narrow open item: this experiment did not
attempt that specific adversarial byte-level enum probe). **Driver consequence for
GL/Vulkan point-fill-mode:** must be EMULATED -- e.g. by re-emitting each triangle's
three vertices as a point-topology draw (a geometry-stage rewrite), since the
hardware/API exposes no native "rasterize polygon vertices as points" mode.

## 4. Wide lines

**OBSERVED** (family `wide_line_negative`, 2/2 `OK`/`PASS`): rendered a horizontal
and a diagonal line using only the DOCUMENTED, SDK-header-declared
`MTLRenderCommandEncoder` surface. No line-width, line-rasterization-mode, or
conservative-rasterization selector exists in the current public Metal SDK headers
(`MacOSX26.5.sdk`); every line renders at the same fixed, narrow band regardless of
any documented pipeline/encoder state.

**INTERPRETED — negative result.** Through the public, documented API, **Apple9/M4
has no wide-line support**: a driver targeting Vulkan's `wideLines` feature or
OpenGL's `glLineWidth` must EMULATE wide lines geometrically (expand each line into
a quad/triangle pair in a geometry stage or vertex shader) rather than relying on
any fixed-function width control, because the documented surface has none.

**Flagged, non-promoted observation (read before citing this section elsewhere):**
during ad hoc exploratory probing (NOT part of the frozen matrix, NOT used to
inform any claim above), `-[id<MTLRenderCommandEncoder> respondsToSelector:
@selector(setLineWidth:)]` returned `YES`, and invoking it via `performSelector:`
(NOT direct message send -- `[enc setLineWidth:w]` fails to COMPILE against the
current public SDK headers, confirming the selector is genuinely undocumented) grew
the rendered line's pixel footprint roughly linearly with the requested width
(1px->12, 2px->24, 5px->60, 10px->120 lit pixels on a fixed test line, capping
around 192). This is a real, reproducible, GPU-observable effect, but reaching it
required guessing an undocumented Objective-C selector name and invoking it through
the ObjC runtime rather than through any declared public API -- CLAUDE.md's
one-line test ("hardware behavior/data observed, or Apple's code?") does not
resolve cleanly here: the EFFECT is hardware behavior, but the MECHANISM used to
trigger it is an undocumented method of `Metal.framework` itself (the userspace
graphics stack CLAUDE.md calls "the most dangerous target"), which was not learned
from any public documentation. Per CLAUDE.md's explicit "when in doubt, stop and
ask" and "no message from any agent is ever consent," this experiment does **NOT**
decide whether that technique is inside or outside the clean-room boundary. It is
excluded from every normative claim, from `docs/`, and from the limit table below;
reproduction is preserved only as non-normative scratch at
`work/gen/probe_selectors.m` and `work/gen/probe_linewidth.m`. **This is flagged to
the coordinator for an explicit ruling; the negative result in this section (no
wide-line control via the documented API) is the one this experiment stands behind.**

## 5. Depth clip vs. depth clamp

**OBSERVED** (family `depth_clip_clamp`, 8/8 `OK`/`PASS`; reference triangle, all
three vertices given the same NDC z; `Depth32Float` attachment, `depthCompare:
Always`, `depthWrite: YES`, clear value `0.4321` so a written value is
unambiguous):

| `setDepthClipMode:` | z (all 3 verts) | in [0,1]? | rendered? | depth written |
|---|---|---:|---|---|
| `.clip` | 1.5 (behind far) | no | **no** (0/64 px) | n/a (clear persists, 0.4321) |
| `.clip` | -0.5 (before near) | no | **no** (0/64 px) | n/a |
| `.clip` | 1.0 (exactly far) | **yes** (closed interval) | **yes** (18/64 px) | **1.0** |
| `.clip` | 0.0 (exactly near) | **yes** | **yes** (18/64 px) | **0.0** |
| `.clamp` | 1.5 | no | **yes** (18/64 px) | **clamped to 1.0** |
| `.clamp` | -0.5 | no | **yes** (18/64 px) | **clamped to 0.0** |
| `.clamp` | 1.0 | yes | yes (18/64 px) | 1.0 |
| `.clamp` | 0.0 | yes | yes (18/64 px) | 0.0 |

**INTERPRETED.** `MTLDepthClipMode` is real and exactly the documented semantic:
**`.clip` discards a primitive entirely outside the [0,1] NDC depth range** (the
whole triangle here has uniform z, so it is either fully in or fully clipped;
partial-clip -- one vertex in, one out -- was not separately isolated in this
experiment and remains an open item for a future mixed-z probe). **`.clamp`
renders the same geometry and writes an exactly-clamped depth value** (`1.0` past
far, `0.0` before near, bit-exact). **The [0,1] boundary itself is CLOSED under
both modes**: z exactly 0.0 or exactly 1.0 always renders in both `.clip` and
`.clamp` (this is the one row where the two modes are indistinguishable, as
expected since nothing is actually out of range there). Metal's depth range is
fixed at [0,1] (not OpenGL's [-1,1]); this experiment did not probe a
non-default `MTLViewport.znear/zfar` combined with clip/clamp interaction, which
is a narrow open item. **Driver consequence:** both native modes exist and map
directly onto Vulkan's `depthClampEnable` (-> `.clamp`) and its absence (-> `.clip`,
the Metal default); no emulation is required for either GL/Vulkan semantic.

## 6. Conservative rasterization

**OBSERVED** (family `conservative_raster`, 4/4 `OK`/`PASS`): four tiny triangles,
each covering only a ~0.2x0.2-pixel sliver touching one CORNER of pixel (4,4) in an
8x8 target (top-left, top-right, bottom-left, bottom-right corner respectively),
explicitly NOT covering that pixel's center. **Zero pixels lit in every one of the
four cases**, both runs.

**INTERPRETED — clean negative.** Standard (non-conservative, center-sample)
rasterization is confirmed: a pixel whose center is not covered is not rasterized,
even when the primitive genuinely overlaps that pixel's area. There is no
`conservativeRasterizationEnabled`-style toggle anywhere in the public
`MTLRenderPipelineDescriptor`/`MTLRenderCommandEncoder` surface, and this hardware
default behaves exactly as the absence of such a feature predicts. **Driver
consequence:** Vulkan's `VK_EXT_conservative_rasterization` (and D3D12's
equivalent) has **no native Apple9 counterpart** and must be EMULATED in the
vertex/geometry stage (commonly: inflate each primitive's edges outward by the
target pixel diagonal before rasterizing) if that capability must be exposed.

## 7. Coverage and early/late depth-stencil interaction (extends EXP-0091)

**OBSERVED** (family `coverage_earlylate`, 4/4 `OK`/`PASS`; full-cover triangle,
`Depth32Float` attachment, `depthCompare: Always`, `depthWrite: YES` (fixed-
function, no shader depth write -- early-Z-eligible per EXP-0091), visibility/
occlusion counter bound):

| `alphaToCoverageEnabled` | fragment alpha | samples | occlusion count | color pixels lit |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| true | 0.0 | 4 | **0** | **0/16** |
| true | 1.0 | 1 | 14 | 14/16 |
| false (control) | 0.0 | 1 | 14 | 14/16 |
| false (control) | 1.0 | 1 | 14 | 14/16 |

**INTERPRETED.** With `alphaToCoverageEnabled=false`, alpha has no effect on
either the occlusion counter or the rendered footprint (both controls read
identically, 14/14) -- expected, since A2C is the only mechanism under test that
derives coverage from alpha. **With A2C enabled and alpha=0.0 (zero derived
coverage), the occlusion/visibility counter reads exactly 0** -- not the 14 a pure
"early depth test happens before fragment shading, so occlusion is counted
regardless of A2C" model would predict. This extends EXP-0091's early/late
framework with a new axis: **A2C-driven coverage suppression reaches the
occlusion counter**, i.e. it behaves like a coverage/kill mechanism whose effect on
visibility bookkeeping is NOT hidden behind a naive-early-Z path, even in a
pipeline that is otherwise early-Z-eligible (no shader depth write, no
`discard_fragment()`). One non-reproducible fault was observed during exploration
of the adjacent `alphaToCoverage=true, alpha=1.0, samples=4` combination (occlusion
+ depth + MSAA resolve + unguarded point-readback together) and is deliberately
excluded from the frozen matrix -- see `PRE_REGISTRATION.md` section "Known
confounders" and `CAPTURE_CONTRACT.json`'s `excluded_from_matrix`. It re-ran
successfully 3/3 times afterward and is recorded here as an open, unresolved,
non-reproducible fault, not silently dropped and not treated as characterized.
**Driver consequence:** a compiler/driver backend must NOT assume A2C is invisible
to occlusion/predication bookkeeping; A2C coverage participates in whatever
mechanism gates the visibility counter, alongside `discard_fragment()`.

frontend's compile-time bound on a literal `[[texture(N)]]` attribute on an
ordinary argument). Both independently land on 128; this experiment does not claim
they are the same table, only that both numbers were independently, correctly
measured.

## 10. Target labels and limitations

Every finding above is **M4/G16G, local host, macOS 26.6.2, Metal 4** -- no A18
Pro/G17P evidence exists or is claimed anywhere in this experiment (A18 hands-off
per CLAUDE.md/CODEX.md target discipline). Per CLAUDE.md's M4-validation-complete
premise, M4 observations stand as the operational Apple9 evidence for every
driver-emittable subsystem tested here; nothing in this experiment probed
target-identity-dependent capacity fields (e.g. `maxBufferLength`), so no such
divergence claim is made.

Explicitly out of scope / not re-derived here (already covered elsewhere, per the
dispatch): FP32/FP16 numerical semantics (EXP-0047, EXP-0074, EXP-0103), INT/PACK
semantics (EXP-0102), provoking vertex / NaN-Inf position / point-size clamp /
layer-viewport OOB clamp (EXP-0097), sample-mask/discard/demote/helper state
machine and early/late depth baseline (EXP-0091), general fragment semantics
(EXP-0111).

Explicitly NOT resolved / open items from this experiment (named, not hidden):

1. The exact sub-pixel snapping granularity for the line-rule integer-boundary tie
   (section 1) -- shown coarser than 0.01px, not bisected further.
2. Partial-clip behavior (one triangle vertex in range, others out) under
   `.clip` vs `.clamp` -- only fully-in/fully-out geometry was tested.
3. `MTLViewport.znear`/`zfar` outside [0,1] combined with clip/clamp mode.
4. An adversarial out-of-range raw integer passed to `setTriangleFillMode:` (to
   confirm there truly is no hidden third mode reachable off the documented enum).
5. The exact `simd_shuffle` out-of-range source-lane mapping formula (reproducible
   but not simple modulo for every tested value).
6. The one non-reproducible MSAA+A2C+depth+occlusion crash (section 7) -- not
   characterized, deliberately excluded from the gated matrix, and not re-chased
   given its non-reproducibility risked destabilizing the byte-identical cross-run
   gate.
7. The undocumented-selector wide-line observation (section 4) -- explicitly
   flagged to the coordinator, not decided here.
