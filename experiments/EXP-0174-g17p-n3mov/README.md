# EXP-0174 — `n3_mov`: does Apple9 have a GPR-to-GPR move, and can we generate it?

**Target:** Apple A18 Pro / G17P (`users-MacBook-Neo.local`), compute stage.
**Status:** COMPLETE. Answer: **yes to both.** See `RESULTS.md` for the one-sentence verdict.

## The question

EXP-0173's closure audit named the first blocker on the whole project:

> No emittable descriptor moves one GPR to a **different** GPR. That blocks `nir_op_mov`, phi
> lowering, parallel copy, register-allocator coalescing and spill reload — which is to say it
> blocks the register allocator, which is to say it blocks the back end.

`n3_mov` was the candidate and its three operand fields — `dst`, `srcA_reg`, `srcA_uni` —
were all `corpus-correlation` (EXP-M4-13, compile-only). This experiment asked whether the
instruction can move one GPR to another, what its operand encoding really is, and whether the
encoding can be **generated** rather than copied.

## Hypotheses and method

Frozen in `PRE_REGISTRATION.md` + `CAPTURE_CONTRACT.json` before any gated case ran
(H1 `dst` is a destination selector; H2 byte+1 is `2*S + half`, not a 7-bit register plus a
uniform flag; H3 byte+2 is an op selector with a release bit; H4 byte+3 selects the
destination half; H5 a full 32-bit copy is generatable as two instructions). H1, H2, H4's
preservation clause and H5 held; H3's and H4's accept masks were **partially refuted and
corrected** by pre-registered falsifiers — see `RESULTS.md` §6.

**Method.** The whole `_agc.main` of our own carrier kernel is replaced by a program we
assemble from `tools/agx-isa`'s own field rules: 16 `mov_imm` seeds (distinct, non-zero) plus
one `falu2i` wide seed, a PRE sentinel written to memory, the four generated bytes under test,
self-restoring padding, a **fixed** 16-register dump, a POST sentinel materialised after the
block, and a 28-word tail poison region — all out of a buffer pre-filled with `0xDEADBEEF`.
**There is no `device_load` anywhere**: DEF-0169-1 established that `device_load` on G17P is
asynchronous and fabricates movement.

Two register plans (`idx15`, `idx7`) are the two carriers; they differ exactly in the
dimension `dst` controls, and are disjoint in both their blind slot (the read-back index
register) and their pad-masked slot, so every one of the 16 GPRs is truly observed in at least
one of them.

## Reproduction

```bash
export SSHPASS=...                    # never written to a file
harness/sync.sh push && harness/sync.sh build && harness/sync.sh verify
python3 harness/run.py  --run g17p_20260830_run01 --order forward
python3 harness/run.py  --run g17p_20260830_run02 --order reverse
python3 harness/grid.py --run g17p_20260830_run01 --plan idx15 --order forward
python3 harness/grid.py --run g17p_20260830_run02 --plan idx7  --order reverse
python3 harness/grid.py --run g17p_20260830_run03 --plan idx15 --order reverse
harness/sync.sh pull g17p_20260830_run01     # ... and run02, run03
python3 analysis/analyze.py --runs g17p_20260830_run01,g17p_20260830_run02
python3 analysis/verdicts.py
```

The instruction under test is **generated**, not spliced: all four bytes are computed by
`isa_helpers.n3_bytes()` from the bit positions `db.json` declares, cross-checked against
`isadb.assemble()` at import time. No byte of it comes from a compiled shader.

## Handover notes for the orchestrator

- `analysis/field_verdicts.json` carries six `hardware-run` rows and **four `db_defects`**
  (DEF-0174-1..4). DEF-0174-1 is load-bearing: `db.json`'s byte+1 field split for `n3_mov`
  (and its `frame_marker` / `mov_zext16` siblings, which share the layout) is one bit off, and
  an emitter following it gets the wrong register *and* the wrong half with no fault.
- **`RESULTS.md` §5 corrects EXP-0168's "r15 is not writable" claim** as a by-construction
  artifact of that experiment's read-back path. EXP-0168's `regs[0] = 0` anomaly is *not*
  reproduced here and is left as an open question about that experiment.
- Nothing in `docs/`, `PROVENANCE.md`, `tools/agx-isa/db.json` or `validation.json` was edited,
  and nothing was committed.
