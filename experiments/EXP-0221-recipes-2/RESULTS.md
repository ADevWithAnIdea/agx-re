## 1. The result that matters most: EXP-0220's blocker was misdiagnosed

EXP-0220's `RESULTS.md` says *"Threadgroup-space stores (`space` bit1) fault against a
device-bound buffer — 4 of 4, both runs. This is the gap that keeps `device_store` at
`generated-no-donor`."* Its own committed raw says something narrower. Its `ds_space` arm
sampled ten values; the four that faulted are 6, 22, 254 and 255, and **`ds_space002` —
`space` bit 1 set, bit 2 clear — came out `ok`/`exact` in both of its runs.**

EXP-0221 swept the field densely on two carriers:

| | values that fault |
|---|---|
| `carrier221.metal` (16 KiB static threadgroup tile) | **64 of 256, exactly `space & 0x06 == 0x06`** |
| `carrier221_notg.metal` (identical, no tile) | **the same 64, byte for byte** |

So **`space` bit 1 alone is legal and always was**; the fault comes from bits 1 and 2
being set *together*, on both carriers, and has nothing to do with whether a threadgroup
allocation exists. My own hypothesis H2 — that the faults were a carrier property — is
**REFUTED by my own control carrier**, and the correct rule is simpler than either of us
had.

## 2. The threadgroup address space, measured

With the tile declared, a generated `device_store` carrying `space = 0x02` does not fault,
does not touch any of the three bound device buffers, and **does write threadgroup
memory**. The proof is an address law that was pre-registered as a boolean prediction
before the run (H3):

> the codeword arrives iff `load_idx_off == 4 * store_idx_off`

| arm | cases | result |
|---|---|---|
| **T3 address law** (8 store offsets × 10 load offsets) | 80 | **80/80 predictions correct** — the 8 pairs `(0,0) (1,4) (2,8) (4,16) (8,32) (16,64) (32,128) (64,256)` delivered, the other 72 were silent |
| **T2 negative control** (identical program, store removed) | 8 | **8/8 silent** |
| **T0 detection power** (same shape, device-space round trip) | 4 | **4/4 delivered into all sixteen read-back registers** |
| **T3 on the control carrier** (no tile) | 80 | **the 8 delivering pairs deliver NOTHING**; 72/80 predictions "correct" only because the silent ones stayed silent |

That last row is the carrier-controlled proof that the datum really is in the threadgroup
tile and not in some other structure: remove the allocation and the round trip disappears
while everything else is identical.

The 4× ratio is the **16-byte store unit versus 4-byte load unit** asymmetry EXP-0100
measured on the M4 by splicing compiler-emitted threadgroup accesses. Here it is measured
on G17P **from bytes we generated**, which is a different and stronger claim: EXP-0100
could only perturb an Apple-emitted instruction, this experiment builds the whole thing.

`base_slot` is the selector: in the 792-case T1 discovery grid **exactly one store
configuration of 792 delivered — `base_slot = 16`** — with `space` 0x02, `access_desc` 0,
`elem_size` 0x11, `st_format` 17, `addr_mode` 0x54.

## 3. Why `device_store` is STILL not canonical — the real reason

**No single generated `device_load` reads the tile back.** The round trip fires only inside
a sixteen-entry load bank whose entries differ from one another; a single load, or sixteen
identical loads, deliver nothing:

| probe | cases | delivered |
|---|---|---|
| gated arm T4, `bank` variant, 0..32 intervening instructions | 7 | **7/7** |
| gated arm T4, `single` (one load, bank slot 5's descriptor) | 7 | 0/7 |
| gated arm T4, `uniform` (sixteen copies of that descriptor) | 7 | 0/7 |
| pilot stage 9 — one load, store descriptor swept densely | 3,336 | **0** |
| post-freeze stage 10 — one load, 6 destinations × 10 `elem_size`, store descriptor swept densely at `base_slot = 16` | 1,596 | **0** |

Two consequences, and both are the reason the verdict does not move:

1. **The store's threadgroup accepted set could not be mapped.** T1 swept the store's
   `space`, `elem_size`, `access_desc`, `st_format` and `addr_mode` densely — but at
   `base_slot = 0`, which the same run then showed cannot deliver. **Those 768 cases have
   no detection power for acceptance**; they are the section-5a pitfall in my own frozen
   matrix, and I am reporting them as uninformative rather than as 768 measurements.
   Stage 10 repeated them at `base_slot = 16` with a single reader and delivered nothing,
   so they remain unmapped.
2. **An implementer cannot emit a threadgroup store from this recipe**, because they could
   not observe it. `device_store` therefore keeps `generated-no-donor`, exactly as
   `PRE_REGISTRATION.md` §9 committed before the first gated dispatch.

**The successor's arm is now one line:** put the sixteen-entry bank in as the read-back
instrument, fix `base_slot = 16`, and sweep the store's five descriptor bytes — 1,280
dispatches. Separately, isolate which property of the bank makes it a reader (its entries
differ in `space`, `access_desc` and `elem_size`; the delivering slots are always the two
with `elem_size = 0x08`, at positions 1 and 5).

## 4. `device_load`: the four fields that had no emitter-grade label

| field | dispatched | distinct encodings | exact | faults | verdict |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| `access_desc` | 256/256 dense | 257 | **256** | 0 | inert; `semantically-mapped` |
| `addr_mode` | 256/256 dense | 257 | **256** | 0 | inert; `semantically-mapped` |
| `reserved7` | 256/256 dense | 257 | **256** | 0 | inert; `semantically-mapped` |
| `reserved13` | 256/256 dense | 257 | **256** | 0 | inert; `semantically-mapped` |

Each case is a complete generated program whose oracle is the full 24-register state plus
every byte of all three buffers, so "inert" here means *the load still delivered
`mem[j]` to the predicted register and nothing else in the machine moved* — not merely
"the output did not change". Both runs agree case for case. The identical four fields on
`device_store` are also 256/256 exact.

`device_store.addr_mode` came out **256/256 exact** where I had pre-registered 128 of them
`corrupt`. That is my error, not the hardware's: I took EXP-0141's *intersection over all
its carriers*, which includes the load-forwarded carrier where bit 1 is required. With an
ALU-computed data source the field is inert over all 256, which is what EXP-0141 says in
prose. **128 of my 415 Gate C failures are that one mistake.** I left them failing.

## 5. `stop.reserved`

| | |
|---|---|
| dispatched | **1,178 of 16,777,216** (0.007%) — all 24 single-bit values and their complements, body byte 0 dense 0..255 twice, bytes 1 and 2 dense, a 128-value deterministic sample |
| distinct actual encodings | 450 |
| halted | **1,178 / 1,178** |
| post-stop tripwire wrote | **0 / 1,178** |
| faults | **0** |

The instrument matters more than the number. `stop` has no destination and no observable,
so an inertness verdict on its body is a check that cannot fail unless something *would*
have been visible had the instruction stopped stopping. Every case therefore emits a
`device_store` of the sentinel register **after** the `stop`, and the paired control
`ctl_tripwire_pre` emits the identical store immediately **before** it. In both runs the
pre-stop control WRITES and the post-stop store does not. That is the detection-power
conjunct §5a demands, and without it these 1,178 cases would prove nothing.

**A pre-registered hazard was refuted.** EXP-0206 records that a control-flow leader in
`stop`'s body byte 0 (`0x0F` / `0x8F`) faults; I froze those six cases as `corrupt`. On
G17P in this carrier **all six executed, halted, and left the tripwire silent** —
`stop_00000f`, `stop_00008f`, `stop_5a5a0f`, `stop_5a5a8f`, `stop_64ed8f`, `stop_6fbe0f`.
They are 6 of my 415 Gate C failures and I did not retro-edit the bucket.

## 6. `device_store.extmode` is a 16-bit HALF-REGISTER index

EXP-0220 left `extmode >= 128` "recorded, not modelled" and noted only that bit 0 "is not a
don't-care". I pre-registered a model from its committed observations (H6: odd values store
two bytes low). **H6 is refuted — 128 of my 415 Gate C failures — and the truth is
cleaner.** With r0..r95 each carrying a distinct codeword, the 16 bytes at the store target
(`target16`) give:

```
extmode   0 -> 0x00000028   r0                    extmode 128 -> 0x42848000  r64
extmode   1 -> 0x00000000   r0.hi | r1.lo << 16   extmode 129 -> 0x00004284  r64.hi
extmode   2 -> 0x41240000   r1                    extmode 130 -> 0x42850000  r65
extmode   3 -> 0x002a4124   r1.hi | r2.lo << 16   extmode 190 -> 0x42940000  r95
extmode   5 -> 0x002b0000   r2.hi | r3.lo << 16   extmode 191 -> 0x00284294  r95.hi | r0.lo << 16
                                                  extmode 192 -> 0x00000028  = extmode 0
```

* **`extmode` indexes the register file in 16-BIT HALVES.** Half `i` is the low half of
  register `i >> 1` when `i` is even and the high half when it is odd. A 32-bit store reads
  the 32 bits *starting at half `extmode`*, so an even `extmode = 2R` delivers r(R) exactly
  (64/64 exact) and an odd one delivers a word straddling two registers.
* **The register index wraps mod 96.** `extmode` 192..255 reproduce 0..63 byte for byte,
  and `extmode = 191` reads r95's high half with **r0's** low half above it — the wrap
  caught in the act. 252..255 fault (r126/r127, confirming EXP-0112).
* **One part is NOT resolved and is reported as unresolved:** the top half of an odd-index
  read follows the next half-register for `mov_imm`-written registers (r0..r15) and reads
  ZERO for `device_load`-written ones (r24..r95). That is operand provenance — §6's
  required dimension — not an encoding bit, and this experiment does not explain it.

## 7. Other results

* **`index_reg` bit 7 is ignored on BOTH the load and the store, including for the
  register-release side effect.** The fault set is identical on both instructions and is
  exactly `index_reg & 0x7F in 96..127` — **64 of 256, i.e. 96..127 and 224..255**, the
  same 64 in both runs. EXP-0141 recorded the mirror for the load on M4; this is the first
  dense G17P measurement and the first for the store. My frozen oracle released register
  `index_reg` rather than `index_reg & 0x7F`, which is why 117 of my 415 Gate C failures
  sit in these two arms — a modelling error whose refutation is itself the finding.
* **`device_store.space` accepts 192 of 256 on G17P**: the 64 rejects are `v & 0x06 == 0x06`
  and nothing else. 64 of the 192 accepted are threadgroup-space stores, which correctly
  leave every device buffer untouched.
* **`device_load.space` faults at 55 of 256 values** in this carrier and accepts the 64
  with `v & 0x03 == 0x00`, matching EXP-0141's exact mask on M4.
* **`device_load.elem_size` accepts 8 values on G17P that EXP-0141 rejected on M4**
  (69, 79, 131, 139, 141, 143, 195, 207) and faults at 32. This is **not** claimed as a
  target difference: with the index register held at 0 the load address is independent of
  the element scale, so acceptance here is a weaker test than EXP-0141's. Recorded as a
  carrier difference candidate.
* **`device_load.base_slot`: bit 7 is ignored.**  Exactly six of 256 values delivered a
  predicted buffer -- `0, 1, 2` and their mirrors `128, 129, 130` -- so all three bound
  slots and all three mirrors, and nothing else.  The other 250 are `unpredicted` and are
  recorded, not claimed (see §11).
* Four descriptor ambiguities recorded for the orchestrator, unchanged from EXP-0220:
  `device_load` with `space` 1 and 17 also satisfies `atomic_mem`/`atomic_rmw`;
  `device_store` with `space` 6 and 22 also satisfies `frag_color_store`/`imageblock_store`.

## 10. How this method could have called a recipe canonical with a donor still present

Three ways it could, and one way it nearly did.

1. **By trusting the provenance tag instead of the bytes.** A tag is a string an author
   types. What makes it evidence is that `census221.py` rebuilds all 14,320 programs from
   the frozen authored inputs and asserts sha256 against raw *before* counting tags.
2. **By reading a required field off our own compiled carrier and calling it "not a
   donor".** This is what EXP-0167 did with `base_slot`, honestly and visibly. The
   threadgroup descriptors are exactly the same trap one level along: the fastest way to
   find them would have been to compile a threadgroup kernel and look at what the compiler
   emitted. That would have made `space`, `access_desc`, `elem_size` and `base_slot`
   CARRIER-supplied for the very class the recipe was missing. They were **measured**
   instead — 792 discovery cases, of which exactly one delivered — and `base_slot = 16` is
   a hardware observation with a run and a case id behind it.
3. **By letting a reader that cannot fail stand in for a measurement.** This one *did*
   happen, in the disclosed pilot, and it is the most useful thing in this record. Pilot
   stage 8 swept the store's every descriptor byte with the sixteen-entry bank as the
   reader and scored "any of the sixteen delivered" as a hit: **1,744 of 1,744 hits**,
   across store descriptors that cannot all be legal. A reader with sixteen chances is not
   a reader. It was caught only because the number was implausible, not because a gate
   refused it — and the same defect is *still present, disclosed, in the frozen matrix*:
   arm T1's 768 descriptor cases sit at a `base_slot` that cannot deliver, so they cannot
   distinguish "this value is illegal" from "this program could never have shown me
   anything". They are reported as uninformative (§3).

And the way it nearly did, which is worth more than the three above: **the blocker itself
was inherited rather than re-measured.** EXP-0220 wrote "threadgroup stores fault, 4 of 4"
and I built an entire carrier, a control carrier and a nine-stage pilot on top of that
sentence. Its own committed raw shows `space = 2` executing cleanly. Ten minutes of reading
the predecessor's raw instead of its prose would have reframed the experiment on day one.
The general form: **a predecessor's RESULTS.md is a claim, and its raw is the evidence; a
successor that only reads the claim inherits its errors and cannot find them.**

## 11. Limitations

* Single-thread dispatch (grid 1, threadgroup 1). Nothing here says anything about
  cross-lane or cross-thread behaviour, including whether the tile is shared as expected.
* `stop.reserved` is a **sample** — 1,178 of 16,777,216 — and no claim of exhaustiveness is
  made.
* Three arms are **oracle-limited** and their `exact` counts are oracle coverage, not
  acceptance: `device_load.extmode` (186 unpredicted), `.ld_format` (28), `.base_slot`
  (250). `analysis/coverage.json` marks each with `exact_is_acceptance: false`; they must
  not be read as accepted-set measurements.
* `device_store.st_format`'s element shapes are recorded in `target16` and NOT decoded here.
* The odd-`extmode` top-half provenance dependence (§6) is unresolved.
* 702 device resets, all self-inflicted, all recovered, both runs measured quiet.
